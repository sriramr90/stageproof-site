The problem

Stage gates get beaten with "TBD", a test contact and a burst of logged calls.

Required fields prove that someone typed something. They do not prove that a decision maker exists, that budget was discussed, or that the buyer is still answering. So the pipeline review spends its thirty minutes on the deals whose stage is fiction, and the forecast inherits the fiction.

A pipeline rule

Validates that a property is non-empty when a deal moves.

budget_confirmed = "Yes" ✓
decision_maker = "TBD" ✓
next_step = "-" ✓

A StageProof criterion

Judges whether the record shows the criterion is true, and cites what it relied on.

budget discussed → unverified (no Budget Holder tagged)
decision maker → met (Maria Chen, contact 7021)
next step → missing ("-" is a placeholder)

Principles

Six rules decide every design question. When two conflict, the earlier one wins.

Evidence over fields

A criterion is met when the record shows it, not when a property is non-empty. "Budget confirmed = Yes" with no email or call mentioning budget is unverified, not met.

Deterministic core, AI at the edges

Rules produce every yes or no that appears in a report. A model compiles criteria, judges evidence and drafts fixes; its output is an input to a rule, never the final record.

Cite or abstain

Every verdict carries the engagement it relied on. Below confidence it says "can't tell" and asks the rep rather than guessing either way.

Rep-first

The rep sees the finding before the manager sees it in a list. A gate that only punishes gets gamed; one that drafts the fix gets used.

Always on, with history

Evaluation runs on every CRM change and every verdict is stored with its timestamp. The product is the record over time.

Never auto-move stages, never silent writes

Writes go only to StageProof's own properties, or to standard properties after an explicit confirm, with who confirmed and which evidence recorded.

Read-only scopes at install. Write scopes only when you turn on confirm-and-write. Email bodies are never requested; StageProof works from subjects, participants, calls, meetings and notes.

Questions
What does StageProof read?

Deals, contacts, companies, owners, pipelines and the engagements already logged in HubSpot: calls, emails (subject, participants and snippet, never the body unless you grant it), meetings, notes and tasks. Only activity logged in HubSpot exists to StageProof, and it tells you which sources your portal actually logs.

What if we do not tag buying roles?

Role criteria read as unverified with the reason, never as missing, and the first audit tells you how many dollars tagging would let it verify. Roles come from HubSpot's association labels or a contact property, never from guessing by job title.

Does it change our data?

At install, no. It creates three of its own deal properties and writes only to those. Writing to standard properties happens only when you enable confirm-and-write, and only when a rep confirms a specific fix. Stage moves are never written.

Where do the criteria come from?

From your own closed deals. Each candidate is tested at the moment deals entered each stage, so it cannot see the outcome, and ranked by how well it separated wins from losses. You see the counts, edit the wording and approve. MEDDIC and BANT templates fill in where history is thin.

Which HubSpot tiers?

The deal properties work on every tier, including Starter, which has no stage rules at all. The deal card and call sources depend on what your tier records.
